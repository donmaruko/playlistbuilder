Art rock is a broad, taste-based umbrella here. Reference artists: Radiohead, Geese,
Black Country New Road, Cameron Winter, Racing Mount Pleasant, Magdalena Bay, and maybe
some Flaming Lips. The common thread: ambitious, left-of-center rock/pop that treats the
song as a canvas. Think experimental structures, unusual instrumentation or production,
dense or theatrical writing, genre-blending (post-rock, art pop, avant-pop, math rock,
chamber/baroque pop, psychedelic, krautrock-adjacent), emotional intensity over
convention. Straightforward pop, hip-hop, country, standard singer-songwriter, and
mainstream rock/metal/punk without the experimental streak are NOT art rock.

## Learned preferences (auto-updated)
- Favor angular, rhythmically inventive band writing (odd meters, jarring guitar lines, tight interplay) that still carries a strong melodic hook.
- Intensity is welcome when it's agile, dynamic and virtuosic (prog or psychedelic freakouts, frantic math-rock); avoid slow-building walls of sound, drone, or crushing heaviness.
- Theatricality should be quirky, neurotic or playful (baroque glam, art-funk, dramatic art pop), not earnest, anthemic or confessional-epic.
- Complex structures are good when they feel composed and controlled; avoid manic, chaotic prog-punk or sprawl that sacrifices song shape.
- Hazy, left-of-center production and psychedelic texture work as a canvas for a real song, but avoid repetitive motorik or hypnotic groove-driven jams.
- Lush baroque or psychedelic arrangements are fine when rock-band-based and moody; avoid orchestral chamber-pop swells and folk-rooted earnest songwriting.
- Vocals should be melodic, expressive or idiosyncratic; deadpan spoken-word delivery and raw, searching singer-songwriter styles don't fit.
- Texture-first, atmosphere-led dream pop is uncertain; prefer songs with clear rhythmic and instrumental drive.
