You're after art rock that's songwriterly and band-driven: sharp hooks, angular or virtuosic playing, and a sense of play, even when the mood is neurotic or intense. Sparks, St. Vincent, Talking Heads, Yes and Fiona Apple all pair unusual structures and rhythms with memorable melodies.

The surprises sit in what you turned down. Mars Volta passed, but Swans and Deafheaven didn't, so intensity works when it's agile and rhythmic rather than a slow, crushing wall. Theatricality is fine from Sparks and Kate Bush, but not from Arcade Fire, Perfume Genius or Cardiacs, which suggests you dislike earnest anthemic swells and chaotic mania. Midlake's lush psychedelia worked, while Sufjan's orchestral approach didn't. Boards of Canada passed, but Can and Stereolab didn't, so hazy texture beats repetitive motorik grooves. Deadpan delivery (Dry Cleaning) also missed, so you seem to want melodic singing or fully committed vocals.

Cocteau Twins skipped, which fits the pattern: texture-forward, with less of the band-driven angularity.

More of: angular guitar-driven art pop and math-leaning rock with strong hooks, quirky or neurotic vocals, and prog or psychedelic flourishes.
Less of: drone, motorik krautrock, deadpan spoken word, and big earnest indie anthems.
